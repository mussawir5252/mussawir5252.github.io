About

How people hold on to dignity and selfhood under pressure.

My creative and research practice examines how people hold on to dignity and selfhood under pressure, whether that pressure comes from the systems around them or from within. I work across narrative cinema, immersive media, and critical video essays, making character-centered work about people who are often overlooked, unheard, or misread: a man worn down by bureaucracy, grief, and financial strain; a painter measuring herself against an impossible standard; a photographer losing their sight.

Cinema became meaningful to me when I first recognized how a simple display of moving images could affect me so profoundly, and that encounter still shapes how I work. I understand filmmaking as a formal and ethical practice of attention. Through performance, framing, duration, sound, and narrative structure, I try to build spaces where an audience can confront the distance between themselves and a character, and then recognize how short that distance is.

I took a Master of Film & Television at Beaconhouse National University in Lahore, summa cum laude, and I am completing an MFA in Digital Media & Design at the University of Connecticut, where I have been Instructor of Record since 2025. Between the two degrees I wrote and directed commercial work in Pakistan and assisted on television drama. I shoot in long takes, so the audience has time to do its own looking. Wherever you are from, I have probably watched a film from your country.

As a filmmaker-scholar I want creative production and research to keep informing one another. My long-term aim is to make films and media projects that are emotionally resonant and critically engaged, and to go on contributing to scholarship on cinematic realism, hybrid forms, and spectatorship.

Films allow us to reflect on our shared imperfections as human beings, a fact that humbles us. This reflection compels us to be more empathetic, teaching us that we all share the same human struggle. A thought which also makes us feel a little less lonely.
