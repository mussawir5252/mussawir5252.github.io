Narrative short · 2026 · writer, director

Pay the Difference

A broke, aging handyman has one Fourth of July to scrape together enough money to stop a funeral home from “disposing” of his dead son’s remains.

A working-class drama about grief, dignity, and the quiet cruelty of institutions. One day. A funeral home, a lawyer’s office, a kitchen, a pawn shop. Shot in Willimantic, Connecticut, and the towns around it, because the story belongs to that landscape.

Nothing should feel expensive except the polished face institutions present to hide their brutality.

Narrative short · 2022 · writer, director, editor · Urdu with English subtitles

Color of Sunset

A photographer takes his student into the hills to teach him how to see. He has not told him it is the last sunset he will ever photograph.

Long takes, an overcast sky, and a Yashica his father gave him. Made as a thesis film at Beaconhouse National University with Chal Phir Cinema, a Lahore collective working to bring audiences back to independent film.

Critical VR Design

A seated VR companion to Pay the Difference. A series of waiting rooms, a funeral home, a lawyer’s office, an insurance counter, where you can sit, look, listen, and do nothing that helps. An experiment in whether removing agency says more about institutions than simulating action does.

02

Both sides

He went to engineering school and came out a filmmaker. Both are still in the room.

The engineer

Musawir Abrar trained as a civil engineer at the University of Engineering & Technology in Lahore. In his third year a friend showed him films from outside Pakistan. One of them was Bergman’s Scenes from a Marriage. He watched it in one sitting and understood his own parents for the first time.

He finished the degree in 2020. He has not built a bridge.

The storyteller

He had been writing since before the degree. A short story placed in a national competition and became a novella. Screenwriting followed, then a master’s in Film & Television at Beaconhouse National University, summa cum laude.

He now writes and directs short films about characters who go unseen, unheard, or misunderstood, and teaches film writing at the University of Connecticut, where he is completing an MFA.

Films allow us to reflect on our shared imperfections as human beings, a fact that humbles us. This reflection compels us to be more empathetic, teaching us that we all share the same human struggle. A thought which also makes us feel a little less lonely.
