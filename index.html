Pay the Difference

A broke, aging handyman has one Fourth of July to scrape together enough money to stop a funeral home from “disposing” of his dead son’s remains.

A working-class drama about grief, dignity, and the quiet cruelty of institutions. Shot in Willimantic, Connecticut, and the towns around it, with a local cast and crew, because the story belongs to that landscape.

Color of Sunset

A photographer takes his student into the hills to teach him how to see. He has not told him it is the last sunset he will ever photograph.

Long takes, an overcast sky, and one sunset. I made it as my thesis film at Beaconhouse National University with Chal Phir Cinema, a Lahore collective working to bring audiences back to independent film.

About

I went to engineering school and came out a filmmaker.

I trained as a civil engineer at the University of Engineering & Technology in Lahore. In my third year a friend showed me films from outside Pakistan. One of them was Bergman’s Scenes from a Marriage. I watched it in one sitting and knew what I wanted to do. I finished the degree in 2020. I have not built a bridge.

I had been writing since before the degree. A short story placed in a national competition and became a novella. Screenwriting followed, then a master’s in Film & Television at Beaconhouse National University, summa cum laude, and a thesis film that traveled to festivals on three continents. Between degrees I wrote and directed commercials and assisted on television drama in Pakistan.

I now write and direct short films about characters who go unseen, unheard, or misunderstood, and teach film writing and film theory at the University of Connecticut, where I am completing an MFA in Digital Media & Design. I shoot in long takes, so the audience has time to do its own looking. Wherever you are from, I have probably watched a film from your country.

Films allow us to reflect on our shared imperfections as human beings, a fact that humbles us. This reflection compels us to be more empathetic, teaching us that we all share the same human struggle. A thought which also makes us feel a little less lonely.
